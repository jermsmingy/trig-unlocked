Angles: degrees vs. radians

Degrees cut a circle into 360 slices, a number someone just picked. Radians measure an angle by how much of the circle's edge it covers, counted in radius-lengths. That's why math (and calculus) prefers them.

🔑 The reference angle trick

The reference angle is the acute angle between the terminal side and the x-axis. Every special angle has the same values as its reference angle; only the signs change.

So you only need to memorize the first quadrant:

θ0\f{π}{6}\f{π}{4}\f{π}{3}\f{π}{2}
sin0\f{1}{2}\f{\r{2}}{2}\f{\r{3}}{2}1
cos1\f{\r{3}}{2}\f{\r{2}}{2}\f{1}{2}0

Pattern for sin: \f{\r{0}}{2}, \f{\r{1}}{2}, \f{\r{2}}{2}, \f{\r{3}}{2}, \f{\r{4}}{2}. Cos is the same list backwards.

SOH CAH TOA & the six trig functions

In a right triangle, the trig functions are ratios of sides measured from the angle θ. Since they're ratios, a bigger triangle with the same angle gives the same values. Try it below.

SOHSin = Opp / Hyp
CAHCos = Adj / Hyp
TOATan = Opp / Adj

Drag the pink handle to change the opposite side and the blue handle to change the adjacent side.

The other three are reciprocals

csc θ = \f{1}{sin θ} = \f{hyp}{opp}
sec θ = \f{1}{cos θ} = \f{hyp}{adj}
cot θ = \f{1}{tan θ} = \f{adj}{opp}

Memory trick: each "co-" function pairs with a non-co function. sec ↔ cos and csc ↔ sin, so every pair has exactly one "co".

The fundamental identities

An identity is an equation that's true for every value of the variable where both sides are defined. These are the tools you'll use to simplify and prove things.

The Pythagorean identity, seen

The triangle inside the unit circle has legs cos θ and sin θ, and hypotenuse 1 (the radius). Pythagoras says:

sin2θ + cos2θ = 1

Divide every term by cos2θ or by sin2θ and you get the other two:

\f{sin2θ}{cos2θ} + \f{cos2θ}{cos2θ} = \f{1}{cos2θ}  ⟹  tan2θ + 1 = sec2θ
\f{sin2θ}{sin2θ} + \f{cos2θ}{sin2θ} = \f{1}{sin2θ}  ⟹  1 + cot2θ = csc2θ

Reciprocal

csc θ = \f{1}{sin θ}   sec θ = \f{1}{cos θ}   cot θ = \f{1}{tan θ}

Quotient

tan θ = \f{sin θ}{cos θ}    cot θ = \f{cos θ}{sin θ}

Even / odd

cos(−θ) = cos θ   sec(−θ) = sec θ even
sin(−θ) = −sin θ   tan(−θ) = −tan θ odd
csc(−θ) = −csc θ   cot(−θ) = −cot θ odd

Why: −θ is the reflection of θ across the x-axis. The x-coordinate (cos) stays the same; the y-coordinate (sin) flips sign.

Cofunction ("co" = complement)

sin(\f{π}{2} − θ) = cos θ
tan(\f{π}{2} − θ) = cot θ
sec(\f{π}{2} − θ) = csc θ
cos(\f{π}{2} − θ) = sin θ
cot(\f{π}{2} − θ) = tan θ
csc(\f{π}{2} − θ) = sec θ

In a right triangle, the two acute angles add to 90°, and the side that's "opposite" for one angle is "adjacent" for the other. So sin of one angle = cos of its complement. That's where the "co" in cosine comes from.

How to type expressions

Use x (or θ) as the variable. Functions: sin cos tan csc sec cot sqrt abs. Powers: sin^2 x or (sin x)^2. Constants: pi or π. Implicit multiplication works: 2sin x cos x, sin 2x. Graphs are strong evidence, not a proof. Use algebra to prove it (see Worked Examples).

Sum, difference, double- & half-angle formulas

These let you find exact values for angles that aren't on the basic unit circle, like 75° or 22.5°, by building them out of angles you already know.

Common trap: sin(α + β) ≠ sin α + sin β. Drag the sliders to see for yourself.

Sum & difference

sin(α ± β) = sin α cos β ± cos α sin β
cos(α ± β) = cos α cos β ∓ sin α sin β
tan(α ± β) = \f{tan α ± tan β}{1 ∓ tan α tan β}

Note the cosine formula flips the sign (∓).

Double-angle

sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ
cos 2θ = cos2θ − sin2θ = 2cos2θ − 1 = 1 − 2sin2θ
tan 2θ = \f{2 tan θ}{1 − tan2θ}

Half-angle

sin \f{θ}{2} = ±\r{\f{1 − cos θ}{2}}    cos \f{θ}{2} = ±\r{\f{1 + cos θ}{2}}
tan \f{θ}{2} = \f{1 − cos θ}{sin θ} = \f{sin θ}{1 + cos θ}

The ± is chosen by the quadrant of \f{θ}{2}.
